Help & keyboard shortcuts

Quick start

  • Import a .docx or .txt file, or paste your script, then press Load into reader.
  • Choose a highlight mode and a speed, then press Start.
  • Click any word in the reading area to set the starting position.
  • Press Record to capture your voice-over while the script scrolls.

About MP3 export

Browsers record audio in WebM or MP4/AAC, not MP3. VoxRead converts your take to a real MP3 using a LAME-based encoder that runs on your device. If the encoder cannot be loaded, VoxRead will tell you and offer WAV or the original format instead — it never renames another format to .mp3.

CEFR pace guide

These are speaking paces, not reading-in-your-head paces. Everything on this platform is built around the rate a learner at each level can actually produce out loud with clear articulation.

  • A1 · 60 WPM — first steps: one short idea at a time, very generous breathing space
  • A1+ · 80 WPM — very slow, every sound given room to land
  • A2 · 100 WPM — slow and clear, short memorised phrases
  • B1 · 120 WPM — steady, still noticeably deliberate
  • B1+ · 140 WPM — conversational; the low end of native speech
  • B2 · 160 WPM — natural native pace (about TED-talk speed)
  • B2+ · 190 WPM — confident presenting, news-broadcast pace
  • C1 · 220 WPM — fluent, fast native conversation
  • C1+ · 260 WPM — rapid delivery; a stretch target, not a daily goal

For reference: IELTS listening recordings run near 140 WPM, audiobooks 150–160, and relaxed native conversation 140–160.

Natural pacing and breath marks

With Natural speech pacing on, the highlight does not crawl at a mechanical constant rate. It holds at punctuation the way a real speaker breathes — briefly at a comma, longer at a full stop, longest between paragraphs. The | and ‖ marks in the script show learners exactly where those breaths fall, and the highlight turns amber while it is holding.

Raise Pause length for A1–A2 learners who need thinking time; lower it for C1 learners working on connected, flowing delivery. Turn the whole feature off for pure metronome drilling.

The WPM number is always the overall pace, measured the way it is in every study of speech: total words divided by total time, silence included. Setting 160 WPM really does deliver the script at 160 words per minute end to end — the words themselves come out a little faster to make room for the breaths. The pacing panel shows both figures, and Your real pace in the dashboard is measured the same way, so it is directly comparable with the target.

Thought groups

Thought groups is the default chunking mode because fluency in speech comes from grouping words into meaningful units, not from producing words one at a time. Word-by-word mode is useful for A1 pronunciation drilling; sentence mode suits confident B2–C1 learners.

Privacy notice

  • Documents are processed locally. Word and text files are read with your browser's own file APIs. They are never sent anywhere.
  • Microphone recordings stay on your device. Audio is captured, mixed and encoded in this browser tab and is discarded when you close it.
  • Music files stay on your device and are only read to play back or mix locally.
  • Files leave only when you choose to download them. Export writes a file to your own computer.
  • No account, no database, no analytics, no tracking.

The only network requests this page can make are the two optional helper libraries listed at the top of its source code (Word import and MP3 encoding), loaded from a public CDN. Once the page has loaded, the app works with no internet connection.
